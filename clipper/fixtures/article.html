Kubernetes eviction, in practice

When a node runs out of memory, the kubelet does not kill the largest pod. It ranks every pod by the quality of service it was given, and evicts from the bottom.

BestEffort goes first, then Burstable above its request, then Guaranteed only if nothing else is left. The eviction manager runs every ten seconds by default, and the thresholds are soft: it reclaims until the signal drops below the threshold, not to zero.

The three settings that matter

Eviction hard is a cliff, eviction soft is a slope, and the grace period is how long the kubelet waits before it stops waiting.

  • memory.available below 100Mi is hard.
  • nodefs.available below 10% is soft.

None of it is a substitute for setting requests, which is what makes the ranking mean anything.
